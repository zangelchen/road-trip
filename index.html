Five ways to spend the weekend. Times are estimates throughout and flexible to what we want to do and when we want to start. Nothing is booked.

Things to do

Everything within reach of the village square, grouped by theme and ordered by how far you have to drive. Mid-October is peak harvest and close to peak colour on Keuka.

Two things that turn on your exact dates. The Watkins Glen gorge trail usually closes for the season around mid-October, and the Harris Hill gliders drop to weekends only in October. Both are worth a phone call the morning of, not an assumption. Drive times below are rough, measured from the village square.

1Wineries, cideries & breweries

Keuka is the small, quiet lake — the west shore is a ten-minute drive with four of the region's best on it. Seneca is a day of its own.

Keuka Lake Wine Trail · tasting passport $45

Tasting flights at six wineries plus discounts, if you are visiting three or more. Mobile only, delivered by text and email. One person per passport, one use per winery, not for large groups, all sales final.

keukawinetrail.com/tasting-passport →
The west shore — under 15 minutes
Dr. Konstantin Frank
8 min

The winery that proved vinifera could survive Finger Lakes winters, and still the benchmark for dry Riesling. The tasting room sits high above the lake, which is the whole point in October.

  • Four generations, founded 1962
  • Rieslings, and the Rückhaus sparkling next door
  • Hilltop terrace — the best foliage view of any tasting room on Keuka
  • Allow 1–1½ hours
Bully Hill Vineyards
6 min

The eccentric one: Walter Taylor's hand-drawn labels, a restaurant with a terrace over the lake, and a free wine museum on the same site. Good for an afternoon rather than a quick flight.

  • Greyton H. Taylor Wine Museum on site
  • Restaurant with lake views — check late-season hours
  • The art and the story are the draw as much as the wine
  • Allow 1½–2 hours with lunch
Heron Hill Winery
10 min

A cathedral-like tasting room on the bluff, with one of the widest views over Keuka. The most photogenic stop on the west shore.

  • Terrace seating when the weather holds
  • Reservations advisable on an October weekend
  • Allow about an hour
Point of the Bluff Vineyards
12 min

Right on the water at the foot of the bluff, with an outdoor amphitheatre. Quieter than the big three and the closest thing to a lakeside tasting.

  • Lakefront setting rather than hilltop
  • Event schedule thins after summer — check what is on
  • Allow about an hour
Domaine LeSeurre
12 min

French winemakers doing restrained, Burgundian-leaning wines. A change of register if three Rieslings in a row is enough.

  • Small and personal — call ahead off-season
  • Allow about an hour
Keuka Brewing Co.
12 min

A small family brewery on the west shore, and the easiest break from tasting rooms without leaving the lake.

  • Flights and growlers
  • Casual, no reservation needed
  • Allow 45 minutes
Around the lake — 15 to 30 minutes
Weis Vineyards
15 min

German-trained winemaking, consistently among the most decorated young producers on Keuka. Worth the extra few minutes north.

  • Riesling and Grüner
  • Small tasting room — check hours
  • Allow about an hour
Steuben Brewing Company
15 min

Hilltop brewery on the west side with a deck facing the lake. Beer, a view, and no tasting-flight formality.

  • Food usually available — confirm late-season
  • Allow about an hour
Keuka Spring Vineyards
20 min

On the Penn Yan end, regularly a Governor's Cup contender. A natural pairing with a Keuka Outlet Trail walk the same morning.

  • Pairs well with the Outlet Trail
  • Allow about an hour
The Seneca side — make it a day, 30 to 50 minutes
Hermann J. Wiemer
30 min

Arguably the most serious Riesling house in the eastern United States, in Dundee on Seneca's west shore. If you make one pilgrimage off Keuka, make it this.

  • Seated tastings — reserve ahead
  • Allow 1½ hours
  • Combines with Watkins Glen the same day
Forge Cellars
45 min

A Rhône producer's project in Hector: dry Riesling and Pinot Noir, spare tasting room, serious wines. Hillside views across Seneca.

  • Reservations recommended
  • Allow about an hour
Boundary Breaks
45 min

Riesling specialists in Lodi with an outdoor tasting deck looking down the length of Seneca. Best on a clear afternoon.

  • Deck seating, weather permitting
  • Allow about an hour
Fox Run Vineyards
35 min

A big, welcoming operation on the west shore with a café and, in autumn, food-and-wine events. On your original list.

  • Check the autumn events calendar
  • Café on site
  • Allow 1–1½ hours
Ravines Wine Cellars
50 min

Dry, food-driven wines from a classically trained winemaker. Geneva end, so pair with Belhurst or a Geneva dinner.

  • Allow about an hour
Belhurst Castle
50 min

An 1880s stone castle on Seneca with a winery, two restaurants and a spa. More of an evening than a tasting stop.

  • Dinner reservations advisable
  • Spa by appointment
  • Allow 2+ hours — treat it as the day's anchor

2Outdoor recreation & fall views

Peak colour on Keuka usually lands mid-to-late October, and the southern end turns first — which is where you are staying.

Glider ride · Harris Hill, Elmira · ~35 min $140

Fifteen to twenty minutes over the ridges in an ASK-21 two-seater with an FAA-certified pilot. Silent, and unmatched for foliage from the air.

October: weekends only10 AM – 6 PMFirst come, first served

For your dates: that means Saturday the 17th or Sunday the 18th — not Thursday or Friday. Weather-dependent, and no reservations, so go early.

In and around the village
Depot Park & the Keuka waterfront
in village

The village green runs down to the lake, with a pier, benches and the old depot. The easiest sunrise or after-dinner walk of the trip — you can do it in slippers.

  • Free, always open
  • Ten minutes on foot from the square
  • Best light early morning
Keuka Lake State Park
15 min

Bluff-top park on the west shore with short trails and open water views down both branches of the lake. The quickest way to a big view.

  • Vehicle fee may apply in season
  • Short, easy trails
  • Allow 1–1½ hours
Garrett Memorial Chapel, Bluff Point
25 min

A tiny stone chapel built into the bluff between Keuka's two arms, with stained glass and a view straight down the lake. The single best foliage stop on Keuka.

  • Free
  • Combine with the Bluff Point drive and Esperanza
  • Allow 30–45 minutes
Keuka Outlet Trail
25 min

Seven flat miles from Penn Yan to Dresden along the old rail bed, past mill ruins and waterfalls. Walk any stretch — the mills near the Penn Yan end are the interesting part.

  • Free, flat, dog-friendly
  • Do a two-mile out-and-back rather than the whole thing
  • Allow 1–2 hours
Worth the drive
Watkins Glen State Park
35 min
Check first

Nineteen waterfalls in a mile of carved gorge, with the stone stairs and bridges that make it the most photographed walk in the region. Autumn leaves collect in the plunge pools.

  • Season risk: the gorge trail normally closes around mid-October — call the park before driving
  • Parking fee in season
  • Roughly 1½–2 hours for the gorge trail
  • Pair with Hermann J. Wiemer, fifteen minutes north
Taughannock Falls State Park
1 hour

A single 215-foot drop, taller than Niagara, in a gorge with 400-foot walls. The rim trail gives the overlook with almost no effort.

  • Free entry
  • Overlook is a five-minute walk from the car park
  • Allow 1–1½ hours
Robert H. Treman State Park
1 h 10

Lucifer Falls and a gorge trail cut with stone steps. Quieter than Watkins Glen and better if the Glen has already closed.

  • A good fallback if the Glen is shut
  • Allow 1½–2 hours
The Keuka loop drive
1–2 h

Routes 54 and 54A around both arms of the lake, over Bluff Point and back. Vineyards, water and hillsides the whole way, with no obligation to stop.

  • Free, and the best use of a grey afternoon
  • Anticlockwise gives you the water on your right
  • Allow 1½ hours with photo stops

3Museums & indoor attractions

Weather insurance. Corning is half an hour south and has two serious museums.

Glenn H. Curtiss Museum
in village

Hammondsport's own: the aviation pioneer who raced motorcycles at 136 mph and built America's first commercially sold aeroplane. Full of restored aircraft, bikes and oddities, with a working restoration shop.

  • Admission charged
  • On the edge of the village — walkable
  • Allow 1½–2 hours
Corning Museum of Glass
30 min

World-class, and far better than it sounds: 3,500 years of glass, live hot-glass demonstrations every hour, and a studio where you can make something yourself.

  • Admission charged; make-your-own projects are extra and book ahead
  • Live glassblowing demos run through the day
  • Allow half a day — it is bigger than expected
The Rockwell Museum
30 min

American art in a Victorian former city hall in Corning — Western and Native American work, a Smithsonian affiliate. Pairs naturally with the glass museum and Gaffer District lunch.

  • Admission charged
  • Ten minutes' walk from the Glass Museum
  • Allow 1–1½ hours
Greyton H. Taylor Wine Museum
6 min

A small, free museum of Finger Lakes winemaking on the Bully Hill site — old presses, cooperage and the Taylor family story.

  • Usually free with a Bully Hill visit
  • Seasonal hours — confirm
  • Allow 30–45 minutes
The Windmill Farm & Craft Market
25 min
Saturday only

A large open-air market near Penn Yan with Mennonite bakers, produce and craft stalls. Autumn is its best season.

  • Saturdays only, and the season ends in autumn — check it is still running for the 17th
  • Cash is handy
  • Allow 1–2 hours

4Dining & the village square

Hammondsport's square is small enough to cover in an evening. The rest is worth a drive.

On the square, on foot
The Park Inn
on the square

The village pub facing the green, and the obvious first stop after checking in. Straightforward food, local wine, and a porch that catches the last of the light.

  • On your original list
  • No reservations usually needed midweek
  • Allow 1–1½ hours
Crooked Lake Ice Cream Parlor
on the square

An old-fashioned soda fountain on the green — sundaes, floats and a lunch counter. Pure village Americana.

  • Seasonal hours — confirm for late October
  • Cheap and quick
  • Allow 30 minutes
Village square tasting rooms
on the square

Several Keuka producers keep small tasting rooms around the green, so you can taste without driving at all. The right move for the first evening.

  • Walkable from anywhere in the village
  • No car needed — the point
  • Allow an evening
Worth the drive
Spotted Duck Creamery
25 min
Call ahead

Duck-egg custard, made on the farm outside Penn Yan. Richer and denser than ice cream because of the yolks — genuinely unlike anything else in the region.

  • On your original list
  • Farm setting, seasonal hours — check before driving out
  • Combine with the Outlet Trail or the Windmill
  • Allow 45 minutes
Esperanza Mansion
25 min

An 1838 Greek Revival house on Bluff Point with a terrace looking down Keuka. Come for the view at sunset rather than for ambitious cooking.

  • Reservations advisable for dinner
  • Pair with Garrett Chapel on the same trip
  • Allow 1½–2 hours
Stonecat Café
45 min
Seasonal

Farm-to-table in Hector on Seneca's east shore, sourcing from growers within a few miles. The clearest expression of what harvest season here actually tastes like.

  • Reserve, and confirm they are still open for the season
  • Pairs with Forge Cellars and Boundary Breaks
  • Allow 1½ hours
Ventosa Vineyards
50 min

Tuscan-styled winery above Seneca with a restaurant angled at the sunset. From your list — go for the last hour of light.

  • On your original list
  • Book a table for sunset
  • Allow 2 hours with the drive back
Fox Run — autumn food & wine
35 min

Their harvest-season food and wine events, from your list. What is on changes week to week.

  • On your original list
  • Check the calendar for 15–18 October
  • Allow 2 hours
Geneva for a night out
50 min

The densest restaurant scene of any town on the lakes, plus Belhurst Castle. The move if you want one proper dinner away from the village.

  • Reservations advisable at weekends
  • Allow an evening

5Niagara Falls — the long day

Roughly 2¾ to 3 hours each way from Hammondsport, so about six hours of driving on top of whatever you do there. It is a whole day, not an excursion. Worth it once if neither of you has been; skippable without regret if you have. Leave by 7:30 AM to be at the falls before the coach parties.
Niagara Falls State Park
2 h 45

The oldest state park in the country, designed by Olmsted, and the free spine of everything on the American side. Prospect Point puts you at the brink of the American Falls; Terrapin Point on Goat Island faces the Horseshoe.

  • Park entry free; parking charged
  • Prospect Point Observation Tower — small fee
  • Goat Island and Three Sisters Islands are the quiet parts
  • Allow 3–4 hours for the park alone
Maid of the Mist
at the falls
Seasonal

The boat into the base of the Horseshoe. Loud, drenching and the reason most people come. Ponchos provided.

  • Season ends late October — confirm it is still sailing
  • Ticketed; expect a queue at midday
  • Allow about an hour with the queue
Cave of the Winds
at the falls
Seasonal

Wooden decks that take you to within a few feet of Bright Bridal Veil Falls. The Hurricane Deck is the wettest you will be all year.

  • Decks come down for winter — check before counting on it
  • Ticketed, ponchos and sandals provided
  • Allow about an hour
Niagara Gorge & Whirlpool State Park
15 min north

Downstream from the falls, where the river turns into a rapids-filled gorge with almost nobody in it. The best walking on the American side, and the foliage is excellent.

  • Free
  • Trails range from a rim stroll to a steep gorge descent
  • Allow 1–2 hours
The Canadian side
Rainbow Bridge

The panoramic view of both falls is from Ontario, not New York — the Horseshoe is best seen face-on from Table Rock.

  • Passport or enhanced licence required for both of you
  • Small toll to cross; walking over is quicker than driving
  • Allow 2+ hours if you cross
Old Fort Niagara
20 min north

An eighteenth-century fort where the river meets Lake Ontario, with buildings that predate the Revolution. A good counterweight if the falls feel too commercial.

  • Admission charged
  • Windy on the point — take a layer
  • Allow 1½ hours

Nothing here is booked. Where a card says to check, it is because the thing genuinely closes or changes in the second half of October — those are the ones to confirm before building a day around them.

Peddler's Village

Peddler's Village, Lahaska, Pennsylvania 18938

A bunch of small shops with a lot of brick path and gardens.

Suggested stay · 1.5–2 h
  • Walk the garden loop. The whole village is a footpath — no traffic, benches everywhere, and planting that gets redone each season.
  • Giggleberry Fair carousel. A restored 1922 Philadelphia Toboggan Company carousel, indoors, running all year.
  • Late lunch. Buttonwood Grill for something proper, or the Cock 'n Bull for a lighter tavern plate.
  • If you're here in fall: the Scarecrow Festival runs mid-September through October.

Virginia Forrest Recreation Area

Virginia Forrest Recreation Area, Delaware Canal State Park, PA-32, Upper Black Eddy, PA

Walk along the Delaware river to enjoy the scenery.

Suggested stay · 1 h
  • Walk the towpath along the river. Dead flat crushed stone with water on both sides. Twenty minutes out and back is enough; the path continues for miles in either direction if you want more.
  • Sit at the river overlook. Picnic tables face the Delaware — bring something from Peddler's Village and eat here rather than in a restaurant.

Jenkins-Lueken Orchards

69 Yankee Folly Rd, New Paltz, NY 12561 · U-pick Sat–Sun 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM

Pick first, eat after. The U-pick fields open at 9 and this is the least crowded you will find them all day.

Suggested stay · 1.5–2 h
  • 20+ apple varieties, with berries too. A wider varietal list than most orchards in the valley, and the picking rows are marked by variety.
  • 2026 U-pick season opened September 5. Running now through the fall, weather and crop permitting.
  • Main U-pick fields: Saturday and Sunday, 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM. Weekends only — worth confirming before a weekday attempt.

New Paltz

New Paltz, New York 12561

Walk a local village and market. Potentially swing by Mohonk.

Suggested stay · 2 h
  • Wallkill Valley Rail Trail along the water. Pick it up where it crosses Main Street and head north across the trestle over the Wallkill — flat, shaded, and the best twenty minutes in town.
  • Mohonk Mountain House grounds to Sky Top. Cut in short version About 30 minutes up from the hotel grounds. Day visitors need a pass, so check availability before driving up.
  • Historic Huguenot Street. Seven original stone houses from the late 1600s on one block.
  • Sojourner Truth Park on the Wallkill. Riverside park at the foot of Main Street, where the rail trail meets the water.
  • Water Street Market. An antiques barn, a bookshop, and outdoor tables at the foot of the ridge.
  • Garvan's Gastropub for dinner if interested. Optional garvans.com/dinner →

Walkway Over the Hudson

Walkway Over the Hudson State Historic Park, Highland entrance · 7:00 AM – 7:30 PM

Only if the day is running early — this is the first thing to cut.

Suggested stay · 1–1.5 h
  • Walk the full span and back. About 45 minutes at a normal pace. The deck is flat and fully accessible; there is no shade and it is windy, so bring a layer.
  • Enter from the Highland side. Closer coming from New Paltz, and the parking lot is bigger. Entry is free; parking runs a few dollars.
  • Open 7:00 AM – 7:30 PM. Last crossing needs to start by about 6:45 to get back across before closing.

Woodstock, Route 100 and Lake Willoughby

Four days on two bases: one night near Woodstock, then two at Lake Willoughby, with the Route 100 foliage drive and the Stowe gondola in between.

🍁 Four-day road trip
Take the Friday off if you can. It is about five hours to Woodstock before stops or traffic. Leaving at 3 or 4 PM means arriving late, eating, sleeping, and having lost the day. Leaving at 9 AM turns Friday into a real first day — Vermont, Woodstock, foliage, dinner — and lets Saturday be the big drive.
